It has been a long time since I last published something on this website! 
I have been quite busy with lots of things over the past year. 
I pretty much stopped working on this project as I started my PhD studies, which took most of my time and brainpower, but I've been wanting to get back to this blog for a long time, and I figured now would be the right time!

So, without further ados, let me get started with the news: Last week, October 14, I successfully defended my PhD with a thesis titled "Probing the Dark Universe: Machine Learning and Statistical Approaches to Gamma-Ray Dark Matter Searches".
I'm extremely grateful to all those who made this project possible: my supervisor, Bryan Zaldivar, for giving me the opportunity to begin this journey, all of my collaborators, my friends, both in Italy and Spain, my colleagues, and my family for supporting me through these 5 long and happy years. 

I have learnt so much of these years, and I believe I've grown both from a personal and professional point of view, and I'm excited to announce that I will continue my journey as a researcher at the University of Valencia in the team of Veronica Sanz, focusing on machine learning methodologies for inverse problems. 

What does this mean for the continuation of this project? As I strive for a better life-work balance, I plan on refreshing the [from zero to julia](https://techytok.com/from-zero-to-julia/) course, with some new pages about the advanced topics I've been learning about. I'm talking about automatic differentiation, programming patterns in julia, artifacts handling, and of course my point of view on how researchers could include AI in their day-to-day work (who would have imagined that the last post I published was before the dawn of the AI era?).

In the meantime, I have been spending A LOT of time learning a language that now sits very close to my heart: JAX. It feels almost like a close sibling to Julia in many aspects, and I find myself using it more and more for every machine learning project I'm working on, so expect something about it too?

Talking about machine learning, last year I published my first substantial solo paper and library: [GenSBI](https://github.com/aurelio-amerio/GenSBI). It's a library for neural density estimation using generative ai, aimed for physicists and in general machine learning scientists, which deserves a dedicated blog post. 

There are a lot of things I want to share, but right now this announcement is already turning into rambling, so I will call it a day as I figure out the new directions I want to explore with this website.

If you are interested in machine learning, numerical methods and simulations, artificial intelligence and generative AI, stay tuned as I share more details on all the projects I've been working on over the past years!
